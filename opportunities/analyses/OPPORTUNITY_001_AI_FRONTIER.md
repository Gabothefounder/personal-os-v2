## T1 — Named-accountability delivery

Sell a **finished result** on a real recurring task. Use frontier agents heavily. Gabriel’s name is on the check. The buyer is not buying “AI.”

### 1. FRONTIER SIGNAL
Agents can now draft, code, summarize, and file-push at a level that looks done. Buyers still need someone who will say “this is actually done” and eat the error. That gap is open *now* and may narrow; the 48-hour test is to see whether anyone will pay a human for the last mile of a task they already have.

### 2. CURRENT MONEY
Operators already pay VA agencies, bookkeepers, researchers, coordinators, junior analysts, and “someone who will just handle it.” They also waste money on SaaS seats that do not finish the job.

### 3. SMALLEST CASH WEDGE
One task, this week, paid only if they would buy it again. Existing tools only.

### 4. NAVAL LENS
- **Game:** Can be a long game if the same class of buyer repeats. Can be a stupid game if it is random gig work.
- **Specific knowledge:** Starts near zero unless the task sits in a domain Gabriel already knows. **UNKNOWN** fit.
- **Ownership:** Weak if it stays hours-for-dollars. Stronger if the residue is reputation, a playbook, and the right to refuse average work.
- **Leverage:** Agents are the labor lever. Gabriel is the judgment lever. Hours can still scale 1:1.
- **Judgment:** The whole product is judgment. One integrity miss (shipping unchecked output) wipes the name.
- **Compounding:** Failure log + repeat buyers. Quitting after two gigs throws it away.
- **Future:** This *is* living in the future — using the tools on real work, not punditry.
- **Freedom:** Cash helps; a full book of delivery jobs is a cage.
- **Good life:** Fine at one job/week. Bad as a hustle identity.

### 5. FRANKLIN LENS
- **Utility:** A task that was not finished is finished. Mud in the street.
- **Cheap experiment:** Yes.
- **Practicality:** Yes — tools already on the desk.
- **Reputation:** Wheelbarrow: industry in fact *and* appearance. Fluent slop destroys credit.
- **Network:** Each buyer is a reciprocal node if the work is real.
- **Institution:** Only if the same job recurs enough to become a company or a standard playbook.
- **Distribution:** Does not spread without Gabriel unless later productized.
- **Independence:** First hundred pounds; then either leisure or capture by more jobs.
- **Public benefit:** Neutral-to-positive if it actually completes useful work.
- **Revision:** If nobody will pay for a finished task, the “AI labor shortage” story is wrong for his reach.

### 6. UNKNOWN DEPENDENCIES
The struggle of doing the task oneself may be how operators still understand their own business. Removing that struggle can destroy tacit knowledge. Function: UNKNOWN until we see an operator who can no longer brief the work.

### 7. COMMODITIZATION ATTACK
The draft, the first pass, the “80%.” If models + agents close the last 20% for *that task*, the delivery fee dies.

### 8. DURABLE LAYER
Named accountability; a log of residual failures; relationships with people who have money and recurring pain.

### 9. ASYMMETRIC INTELLIGENCE
Where current agents actually break on live work — not on benchmarks. Most outsiders only see demos.

### 10. 48-HOUR TEST
See recommended experiment at the end. This territory *is* that test.

### 11. KILL CONDITION
20 reachable people, no one will name a real task; or a delivered task they would not pay $50 for; or >8 hours of Gabriel time with no plausible lever.

### 12. UPSIDE PATH
Service → weekly workflow for one buyer type → failure/data log + reputation → product/process others run → acquire or be acquired as a specialist operator, not as a wrapper.

### 4. NAVAL LENS

- **Leverage:** Low until the playbook runs without him.
- **Judgment:** Domain-mismatch ruin if he implies licensed expertise he does not have.
- **Compounding:** Deep in one vertical. Reset if he hops professions.
- **Future:** Yes, if that profession is still economically alive when average labor is cheap.
- **Freedom:** Vertical expertise can trap *or* price independence.
- **Good life:** Depends entirely on whether he likes those people.

### 5. FRANKLIN LENS
- **Utility:** Only after the profession’s nuisance is named.
- **Cheap experiment:** One practitioner, 48 hours, one loop.
- **Practicality:** Needs access to real files. That is the hard part.
- **Reputation:** Lasting if useful; fatal if he looks like a tourist.

- **Institution:** Vertical standard, shared library of procedures.
- **Distribution:** Other shops in the trade if the playbook is copyable.
- **Independence:** High if they pay; dependency if they become his only buyer type and then consolidate.
- **Public benefit:** If the profession serves the public better/cheaper.
- **Revision:** First three practitioners bored or scared of file-sharing → kill the vertical, not “try harder messaging.”

### 6. UNKNOWN DEPENDENCIES

### 8. DURABLE LAYER

### 9. ASYMMETRIC INTELLIGENCE

### 10. 48-HOUR TEST

### 5. FRANKLIN LENS

- **Practicality:** Yes.

- **Network:** Repeat senders.

### 7. COMMODITIZATION ATTACK

### 10. 48-HOUR TEST

### 11. KILL CONDITION

### 12. UPSIDE PATH

### 5. FRANKLIN LENS

### 3. SMALLEST CASH WEDGE
