# Benjamin Franklin

Status: deep library, ingestion #1 (2026-08-31)
Lens (derived, keep short): `wisdom/lenses/FRANKLIN.md`

Not a quote poster and not a claim that Franklin is always right. He records **errata** on purpose.

Primary corpus for this ingestion:

- *The Autobiography of Benjamin Franklin* (written 1771–1790; Gutenberg ed. used here): https://www.gutenberg.org/files/20203/20203-h/20203-h.htm
- *Advice to a Young Tradesman* (1748)
- *The Way to Wealth* / Father Abraham (preface to *Poor Richard Improved*, 1758)
- Letter to Joseph Priestley, 19 September 1772 (“moral or prudential algebra”) — widely reprinted from the Franklin Papers / Founders Online tradition
- Junto / Library Company as described in the Autobiography and Library Company of Philadelphia institutional history of the 1731 Articles of Agreement

The Autobiography **does not** cover the French mission (1776–1785) in any usable depth. Do not treat later diplomatic folklore as sourced here.

## 1. Practical experimentation

### FRANKLIN-001
PRINCIPLE: Begin public work with a small, visible local defect, not a constitution.
SOURCE: Autobiography, “Interest in Public Affairs” — city watch, then fire
SOURCE CLAIM: He turned to public affairs “beginning, however, with small matters.” First: the watch (unequal six-shilling tax, ragamuffin substitutes, tippling). Paper to Junto → other clubs “as arising in each of them” → later law. Then a paper on fire accidents → Union Fire Company with buckets, monthly meetings, fines buying engines. Fifty years later the first company still existed; fires limited to one or two houses.
CONTEXT: Philadelphia municipal failure.
APPLICATION: Prototype the smallest recurring harm; let copies spawn.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: Needs a network that can copy (Junto → other clubs).
TENSION: FRANKLIN-039 (durable institutions) vs never-ending tinkering.
COUNTERARGUMENT: Small starts can be captured by the same interests that caused the defect.

### FRANKLIN-002
PRINCIPLE: Prepare minds before asking for money or law.
SOURCE: Autobiography — academy (1749)
SOURCE CLAIM: Wrote *Proposals Relating to the Education of Youth in Pennsylvania*, distributed gratis, “as soon as I could suppose their minds a little prepared,” then subscription in yearly quotas for five years so the subscription might be larger.
CONTEXT: Founding what became the Academy / University of Pennsylvania.
APPLICATION: Circulation of a plain proposal is part of the experiment.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: Whitefield emptied his pocket *against* a prior resolution — oratory can smash preparation.
TENSION: FRANKLIN-033 (persuasion can override one’s own plan).
COUNTERARGUMENT: Delay lets opponents organize.

### FRANKLIN-003
PRINCIPLE: Hide personal authorship of public schemes when credit would block adoption.
SOURCE: Autobiography, academy introduction
SOURCE CLAIM: Stated publication “not as an act of mine, but of some publick-spirited gentlemen,” “avoiding as much as I could, according to my usual rule, the presenting myself to the publick as the author of any scheme for their benefit.”
CONTEXT: Getting subscriptions for the academy.
APPLICATION: If the inventor’s name is a tax on the idea, remove the name.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: Conflicts with tradesman credit that *requires* a visible name (wheelbarrow).
TENSION: FRANKLIN-010 (tradesman credit requires a visible name).
COUNTERARGUMENT: Anonymous schemes evade accountability.

## 2. Self-education

### FRANKLIN-004
PRINCIPLE: Education is self-directed, cheap, and social—not waiting for a college.
SOURCE: Autobiography — reading, Junto, Library Company
SOURCE CLAIM: Left school early; trained as printer; formed Junto 1727 for queries on morals, politics, natural philosophy and quarterly essays. Books scarce and expensive; pooling created the subscription library (1731).
CONTEXT: Artisan Philadelphia.
APPLICATION: A learning club can precede an institution.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: He later founds an academy—self-education is not anti-school.
TENSION: FRANKLIN-041 (formal academy).
COUNTERARGUMENT: Autodidacts inherit print-culture privilege he then democratizes.

## 3. Discipline and habits

### FRANKLIN-005
PRINCIPLE: Speculative conviction of virtue is not enough; contrary habits must be broken and good ones established.
SOURCE: Autobiography, moral-perfection project
SOURCE CLAIM: He thought knowing right/wrong would suffice; habit and inclination beat reason. Designed a method: thirteen named virtues, one at a time, daily marks in a little book.
CONTEXT: Young tradesman’s character project.
APPLICATION: One behavior, tracked, sequenced (temperance first for a clear head).
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: He never claimed perfection; Order was a lasting failure; Humility he got mostly as *appearance*.
TENSION: FRANKLIN-019 (curiosity/books “debauch’d” him from work).
COUNTERARGUMENT: Virtue charts can become moral bookkeeping theater.

## 4. The 13 virtues

### FRANKLIN-006
PRINCIPLE: Use more names with fewer ideas each, plus a one-line precept, rather than vague mega-virtues.
SOURCE: Autobiography, list of thirteen
SOURCE CLAIM: Temperance through Humility with short precepts (eat not to dullness; speak not but what may benefit; waste nothing; lose no time; no hurtful deceit; imitate Jesus and Socrates; etc.). Sequence: Temperance → Silence → Order → Resolution, then Frugality/Industry to enable Sincerity and Justice.
CONTEXT: Reading too-broad catalogues of virtue.
APPLICATION: Decision checklists should be operational, not sermon titles.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: Chastity/humility precepts are period-bound; don’t import them as a modern HR code.
TENSION: Sincerity vs “no hurtful deceit” leaving room for useful silence and appearance.
COUNTERARGUMENT: Fragmenting virtue can miss character as a whole.

### FRANKLIN-007
PRINCIPLE: Humility was added late; he succeeded more at the appearance than the reality.
SOURCE: Autobiography, Humility section
SOURCE CLAIM: Could not boast much success in acquiring the *reality* of humility, but a good deal with the *appearance*: forbade “certainly/undoubtedly,” used “I conceive / I apprehend / I imagine / it appears to me”; began replies by granting the other might be right in some cases. Conversations became more pleasant and persuasive.
CONTEXT: A friend told him he was proud; thirteenth virtue added.
APPLICATION: Modest diction as a tool—and as a known gap between inner pride and outer manner.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: He admits the appearance/reality split himself.
TENSION: FRANKLIN-043 (Socratic entrapment he later dropped); FRANKLIN-010 (appearance of industry).
COUNTERARGUMENT: Managed humility is manipulation (he almost says so).

## 5. Moral algebra / decision-making

### FRANKLIN-008
PRINCIPLE: Hard decisions are hard because pros and cons are not present to the mind at the same time.
SOURCE: Franklin to Joseph Priestley, 19 Sept 1772
SOURCE CLAIM: He will not advise *what* to decide without premises, but *how*: two columns, Pro/Con; three or four days of short hints as they occur; then estimate weights; strike equal pairs (or 1=2, 2=3); wait another day or two; then determine. Weights are not algebraic-precise, but the equation of “moral or prudential algebra” reduced rash steps.
CONTEXT: Priestley asked advice on a career/office decision (Shelburne librarian offer in the surrounding correspondence).
APPLICATION: Separate collection of reasons from the moment of weighting; no fake numeric score.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: He still uses judgment of “respective weights”—the method organizes bias, it does not delete it.
TENSION: FRANKLIN-033 (Whitefield emptied his gold against a prior resolution in minutes).
COUNTERARGUMENT: Column-striking can launder a decision already made.

## 6. Reputation and credit

### FRANKLIN-009
PRINCIPLE: Credit is money; punctual payment commands other people’s purse.
SOURCE: *Advice to a Young Tradesman*, 1748
SOURCE CLAIM: Time is money (idle half-day costs the wage, not just the sixpence spent). Credit is money (others’ funds in your hands earn as if yours). Money begets money. “The good paymaster is lord of another man’s purse.” Never keep borrowed money an hour beyond the promise. After industry and frugality, punctuality and justice raise a young man. The sound of the hammer at five and nine is watched; trifles affect credit.
CONTEXT: Counsel to a young tradesman.
APPLICATION: Reliability is a capital asset, not etiquette.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: Reputation can be *performed* (wheelbarrow) as well as earned.
TENSION: FRANKLIN-010.
COUNTERARGUMENT: Credit systems also enable predation and debt peonage.

### FRANKLIN-010
PRINCIPLE: Be industrious in fact *and* avoid appearances to the contrary.
SOURCE: Autobiography, after taking the printing house
SOURCE CLAIM: To secure credit and character: dressed plainly; no idle diversion; rarely fishing/shooting; books “snug”; sometimes brought paper home through the streets on a wheelbarrow. Esteemed industrious; stationers solicited him; Keimer’s credit collapsed.
CONTEXT: Young printer needing merchant trust.
APPLICATION: Stakeholders underwrite what they can *see*.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: Appearance management vs authenticity; he wanted both reality and show.
TENSION: FRANKLIN-007 (appearance of humility); FRANKLIN-043.
COUNTERARGUMENT: Branding industry can hide extraction.

## 7. Business and commerce

### FRANKLIN-011
PRINCIPLE: Put every partner duty in writing while esteem is high; partnerships otherwise end in quarrels.
SOURCE: Autobiography, printing partnerships in other colonies
SOURCE CLAIM: Promoted workmen who behaved well into printing houses on explicit articles; most did well and bought the types after six years. “Partnerships often finish in quarrels”; his ended amicably because everything expected of each partner was settled in the articles—jealousies and unequal burden otherwise bring lawsuits.
CONTEXT: Scaling a print network.
APPLICATION: Founder warmth is not a contract.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: Meredith: he stayed too long from obligation while the partner was “seldom sober.”
TENSION: FRANKLIN-012 (loyalty vs cutting a bad partner).
COUNTERARGUMENT: Over-specified contracts can destroy flexibility.

### FRANKLIN-012
PRINCIPLE: Do not dissolve a partnership while the other party can still perform; accept help only after they fail.
SOURCE: Autobiography, Meredith crisis
SOURCE CLAIM: Father unpaid a hundred pounds; suit pending. Coleman and Grace separately offered to buy him out of Meredith. He refused while any prospect remained of Meredith fulfilling the agreement, from obligation; if they finally failed, then he would accept. Later bought Meredith out when the father could not pay.
CONTEXT: Printing house nearly sold at half price.
APPLICATION: Honor the deal until it is actually broken—then move.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: Friends already saw Meredith as discredit in the streets.
TENSION: FRANKLIN-009 (credit destroyed by a drunk partner) vs obligation.
COUNTERARGUMENT: Waiting for “final failure” can wipe both.

### FRANKLIN-013
PRINCIPLE: After the first hundred pounds, the second is easier; money is “prolific.”
SOURCE: Autobiography; *Advice to a Young Tradesman*
SOURCE CLAIM: Newspaper profitable, almost the only one in the neighboring provinces; “after getting the first hundred pound, it is more easy to get the second.” Same compounding image as breeding money in 1748 advice.
CONTEXT: Gazette success.
APPLICATION: Early capital and distribution compound.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: He also lost years to Keith’s fake credit and London dissipation.
TENSION: FRANKLIN-014 (frugality) vs later public leisure captured by office.
COUNTERARGUMENT: Path-dependence and monopoly of the Gazette, not a law of nature.

## 8. Frugality and capital accumulation

### FRANKLIN-014
PRINCIPLE: The way to wealth is industry and frugality—waste neither time nor money—unless Providence determines otherwise.
SOURCE: *Advice to a Young Tradesman*; *The Way to Wealth* (1758)
SOURCE CLAIM: Without industry and frugality nothing will do; with them everything. Get honestly, save all except necessary expenses. Father Abraham stitches Poor Richard maxims against complaining of taxes: the issue is also idleness and luxury.
CONTEXT: Tradesman advice; almanac preface as popular sermon.
APPLICATION: Cut leak before seeking leverage.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: Closing clause: God may “otherwise determine.” Poor Richard is also a *persona* and a product.
TENSION: FRANKLIN-031 (refuse patent); FRANKLIN-015 (independence via fortune, then offices eat leisure).
COUNTERARGUMENT: Structural taxes, slavery, and inheritance—not only personal vice—shape wealth; almanac moralism can blame the poor.

## 9. Independence and freedom

### FRANKLIN-015
PRINCIPLE: Frugality and industry were means to independence, which was supposed to buy leisure for science—public office then seized the leisure.
SOURCE: Autobiography, after leaving private business
SOURCE CLAIM: “Sufficient tho’ moderate fortune” for philosophical studies; bought Spence’s electrical apparatus; then “the publick, now considering me as a man of leisure, laid hold of me”—justice of the peace, council, alderman, Assembly burgess.
CONTEXT: Retirement from printing into experiment—then capture by civic demand.
APPLICATION: Independence is unstable if you are useful.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: He also *sought* influence (clerk rule: never resign).
TENSION: FRANKLIN-044 (never ask, never refuse, never resign).
COUNTERARGUMENT: “Leisure stolen by the public” is also how elites monopolize office.

## 10. Networking / Junto

### FRANKLIN-016
PRINCIPLE: A club of mutual improvement is infrastructure: queries, essays, no victory-debating, fines for heat.
SOURCE: Autobiography, Junto (1727)
SOURCE CLAIM: Friday evenings; queries on morals, politics, natural philosophy; essay every three months; president; “sincere spirit of inquiry after truth, without fondness for dispute, or desire of victory”; later, positiveness and direct contradiction contraband under small fines.
CONTEXT: Young tradesmen without a university.
APPLICATION: Design the conversational rules or the group becomes a status fight.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: Members also steered printing jobs to him (Breintnal, Quaker history).
TENSION: FRANKLIN-017 (network as market) vs disinterested inquiry.
COUNTERARGUMENT: Clubs exclude; “mutual improvement” can be a cartel.

### FRANKLIN-017
PRINCIPLE: Pool capital when no one can buy the good alone.
SOURCE: Autobiography + Library Company Articles of Agreement, 1 July 1731
SOURCE CLAIM: Few readers, most poor; sketch of plan; conveyancer Brockden; subscribe a sum down plus annual contribution. Library Company: shareholders, books for all subscribers. Seal motto (institutional): pour forth benefits for the common good.
CONTEXT: Imported books expensive.
APPLICATION: Subscription / mutualization as the first product.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: Still a shareholders’ library, not purely free to all at founding.
TENSION: Commercial printing profits vs common-good library.
COUNTERARGUMENT: Mutuals can stagnate without new capital.

## 11. Reciprocity

### FRANKLIN-018
PRINCIPLE: Fix obligation on earth, not in heaven, if you want a real friendship.
SOURCE: Autobiography, Whitefield lodging
SOURCE CLAIM: Offered his house. Whitefield: if for Christ’s sake, a reward. Franklin: “Don’t let me be mistaken; it was not for Christ’s sake, but for your sake.” Joke: saints shift the burden to heaven; he “contriv’d to fix it on earth.”
CONTEXT: Civil friendship with a preacher who prayed for his conversion.
APPLICATION: Name the actual counterparty in the gift.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: He still emptied his pocket at the sermon.
TENSION: FRANKLIN-033.
COUNTERARGUMENT: Reducing gifts to dyadic debt can cheapen solidarity.

## 12. Persuasion

### FRANKLIN-019
PRINCIPLE: Dogmatic manner defeats informing, being informed, pleasing, and persuading.
SOURCE: Autobiography, after Socratic phase
SOURCE CLAIM: Ends of conversation: inform or be informed, please or persuade. Positive manner provokes contradiction, drives modest people away, leaves you in your error. Pope: teach as if you taught them not; speak with seeming diffidence.
CONTEXT: After dropping entrapment questioning.
APPLICATION: Soften assertion if you actually want a measure passed.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: He used the same manner to *inculcate opinions* and promote measures—persuasion, not neutrality.
TENSION: FRANKLIN-032 (early delight in victories “neither myself nor my cause always deserved”).
COUNTERARGUMENT: Crisis sometimes needs blunt speech.

### FRANKLIN-020
PRINCIPLE: Print multiplies persuasion—and freeze-frames error. *Litera scripta manet.*
SOURCE: Autobiography, Whitefield’s writings
SOURCE CLAIM: Unguarded spoken opinions can be qualified or denied; writing cannot. Critics used Whitefield’s print to diminish votaries. Franklin’s opinion: if he had never written, the sect and posthumous reputation might have grown by enthusiastic feigning.
CONTEXT: Evangelist as media case.
APPLICATION: What you publish becomes the attack surface.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: Franklin’s own career is print.
TENSION: FRANKLIN-036 (distribution is power).
COUNTERARGUMENT: Without writing, ideas die with the speaker; this is anti-science.

## 13. Diplomacy

### FRANKLIN-021
PRINCIPLE: When you cannot get a law from the Assembly, try a voluntary association and let numbers do the arguing.
SOURCE: Autobiography, *Plain Truth* and Associators (1747)
SOURCE CLAIM: Quaker Assembly would not pass a militia law. He published *Plain Truth*, then an instrument of association, meeting in the large hall, pens distributed; 1,200 hands that day, >10,000 later. Lottery for a battery. Borrowed cannon from NY’s Clinton as Madeira softened him from six to eighteen. Took his turn as a common soldier. Declined colonel, recommended Lawrence.
CONTEXT: Defense of Pennsylvania.
APPLICATION: Coalition outside the blocking institution.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: Peace soon intervened; Quaker politics remained.
TENSION: FRANKLIN-024 (keep the clerkship) vs offending the majority sect.
COUNTERARGUMENT: Volunteer militias can be extra-legal power.

### FRANKLIN-022
PRINCIPLE: Discover the real preference of a blocking group by how they vote with their feet, not their published principle.
SOURCE: Autobiography, fire-company lottery for the battery
SOURCE CLAIM: Twenty-two of thirty members Quakers. Morris said Friends were all against. Two Quakers downstairs: eight would vote with Franklin if needed but hoped not to be called, to avoid elders. He delayed an hour, then 8–1; estimated sincere opponents among that society as 1 to 21 by absence.
CONTEXT: Defense vs Quaker peace testimony.
APPLICATION: Published principle ≠ median member.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: Secret side-deals can be seen as trickery.
TENSION: FRANKLIN-043 (persuasion as entanglement) and the Sincerity precept (“use no hurtful deceit”).
COUNTERARGUMENT: Counting absences as consent is a political trick.

### FRANKLIN-023
PRINCIPLE: Do not dispute a professional on his turf in public if you still need the relationship—and still log the forecast.
SOURCE: Autobiography, Braddock
SOURCE CLAIM: Doubted the long thin line through woods; “ventur’d only to say” the only danger he apprehended was Indian ambush on a four-mile slender line. Braddock smiled at American ignorance; regulars could not be impressed by “savages.” Franklin, “conscious of an impropriety in disputing with a military man in matters of his profession,” said no more. The enemy did **not** take the long-line advantage he feared; they attacked nine miles from the fort when the army was more in a body. He had still refused to subscribe a firework for taking Duquesne beforehand: “events of war are subject to great uncertainty.”
CONTEXT: Wagon contract and imperial campaign, 1755.
APPLICATION: Modest dissent + written memory vs winning the room.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: Silence can be complicity in disaster.
TENSION: FRANKLIN-019 (persuade) vs professional deference.
COUNTERARGUMENT: He still organized wagons for a campaign he doubted.

## 14. Negotiation

### FRANKLIN-024
PRINCIPLE: Never ask for an office, never refuse one, never resign one—add reprisal rights if they take it.
SOURCE: Autobiography, threat of losing Assembly clerkship
SOURCE CLAIM: Young man advised resigning honorably. Franklin: public man who never asked, never refused; he adds never resign. If they take the clerkship, they shall take it from him, preserving right of reprisal. He was unanimously chosen again.
CONTEXT: Fear that militia activity offended Quakers.
APPLICATION: Don’t self-displace to look honorable.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: Sounds like attachment to office, not independence.
TENSION: FRANKLIN-015 (wanted scientific leisure).
COUNTERARGUMENT: Never-resign can become a spoils ethic.

### FRANKLIN-025
PRINCIPLE: Use the other side’s incentives (wine, religion, lotteries, printed forms) as the closing mechanism.
SOURCE: Autobiography — Clinton’s Madeira; fast proclamation; lottery for battery; printed association copies
SOURCE CLAIM: Cannon count rose with bumpers. Fast drawn in New England style, German translation, clergy influencing congregations. Lottery filled expeditiously.
CONTEXT: Defense logistics.
APPLICATION: Design the yes-path in the room.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: Close to manipulation; Clinton was drunk.
TENSION: Sincerity precept vs closing tactics; FRANKLIN-043.
COUNTERARGUMENT: Manufacturing consent.

## 16. Institution building

### FRANKLIN-027
PRINCIPLE: Do not freeze a creed in print if you expect further light—printed confession binds successors.
SOURCE: Autobiography, Dunkers (Michael Welfare)
SOURCE CLAIM: Welfare would not publish articles of faith: they had already reversed errors; more light might come; print would make them unwilling to improve, successors treating founders as sacred. Franklin: perhaps unique modesty; others assume they hold all truth—fog metaphor: nearby looks clear.
CONTEXT: Sects and written doctrine.
APPLICATION: Institutions need revision rules, not only founding documents.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: Unwritten groups can hide abuse.
TENSION: FRANKLIN-011 (write partner duties explicitly).
COUNTERARGUMENT: Without a charter, institutions dissolve or get captured.

### FRANKLIN-028
PRINCIPLE: Seed an institution with a coalition of active friends (often Junto), a pamphlet, and divisible subscriptions.
SOURCE: Academy 1749; Philosophical Society 1744; Library 1731
SOURCE CLAIM: 1743 academy proposal lay dormant when Peters declined; 1744 succeeded with APS. Academy: associate friends, pamphlet, five-year quotas.
CONTEXT: No college, no militia—two regrets about Pennsylvania.
APPLICATION: Repeatable founding stack.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: Needs his print shop and reputation.
TENSION: Individual credit vs “publick-spirited gentlemen.”
COUNTERARGUMENT: Institutional founding by a printer-politician is not a general citizen skill.

## 17. Distribution / printing / media

### FRANKLIN-029
PRINCIPLE: A newspaper is both a business and a civic channel; anonymity can launch a writer and wound a brother.
SOURCE: Autobiography, Silence Dogood / Courant
SOURCE CLAIM: Slipped anonymous papers under the door; judges attributed them to men of learning; brother displeased when vanity and authorship emerged. Indenture “erratum”: asserted freedom unfairly.
CONTEXT: Boston apprenticeship.
APPLICATION: Media distribution can precede a brand—and create family errata.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: Deception of James Franklin.
TENSION: FRANKLIN-036 (errata named later); anonymity vs later tradesman visibility.
COUNTERARGUMENT: Ghostwriting as origin myth excuses later opacity.

### FRANKLIN-030
PRINCIPLE: Poor Richard is a distribution machine for maxims—useful, and also a commercial persona.
SOURCE: *Poor Richard* / *The Way to Wealth* (1758)
SOURCE CLAIM: Father Abraham at an auction, stitching almanac sayings against tax-ruin talk. Franklin frames gratification at being quoted. The almanac made the printer’s fortune as well as a moral brand.
CONTEXT: Annual print product.
APPLICATION: Repeatable short counsel at scale.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: Do not treat every proverb as Franklin’s private practice (he records luxury, London plays, errata).
TENSION: FRANKLIN-014 vs lived errata.
COUNTERARGUMENT: Maxims flatten political economy into character advice.

## 18. Science and invention

### FRANKLIN-031
PRINCIPLE: We enjoy others’ inventions; we should serve others with ours freely—decline the patent.
SOURCE: Autobiography, Pennsylvania fireplace, 1742
SOURCE CLAIM: Model to Robert Grace; pamphlet explaining construction and answering objections; Governor offered a patent; declined: as we enjoy advantages from others’ inventions, we should serve freely and generously. London ironmonger patented a worse copy and made a “little fortune”; Franklin did not contest, hating disputes and not wanting patent profit. Stoves saved wood in the colonies.
CONTEXT: Heating invention.
APPLICATION: Public-domain infrastructure vs rent.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: Others captured the London rent; “hating disputes” is also conflict-avoidance.
TENSION: FRANKLIN-009 (credit/capital) vs gift of IP.
COUNTERARGUMENT: Without patents, copyists with capital win; inventors starve.

### FRANKLIN-032
PRINCIPLE: Measure what others treat as legend.
SOURCE: Autobiography, Whitefield’s voice
SOURCE CLAIM: Walked backward down Market Street to estimate how far the voice carried; semicircle and two square feet per auditor → >30,000; reconciled newspaper claims of 25,000 and ancient army harangues he had doubted.
CONTEXT: Curiosity during a sermon.
APPLICATION: Replace awe with a back-of-envelope observation.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: Rough geometry is not a lab.
TENSION: FRANKLIN-033 (the same sermon emptied his gold).
COUNTERARGUMENT: Quantifying crowds can still be self-deception.

## 19. Curiosity

### FRANKLIN-033
PRINCIPLE: Curiosity includes being the specimen: he resolved to give Whitefield nothing, then gave copper, then silver, then all the gold.
SOURCE: Autobiography, Whitefield collection
SOURCE CLAIM: Disapproved building the orphan house in Georgia vs Philadelphia; refused to contribute; attended a sermon intending to give nothing; oratory moved him stepwise to empty the pocket. A friend had emptied pockets in advance and could not borrow from the one unmoved man: “thee seems to be out of thy right senses.”
CONTEXT: Charity vs his operational objection.
APPLICATION: Notice when rhetoric overrides your prior experiment design.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: He still judged Whitefield personally honest (printed his journals).
TENSION: FRANKLIN-005 (resolution); FRANKLIN-008 (multi-day algebra).
COUNTERARGUMENT: The story flatters his later irony more than it instructs.

### FRANKLIN-034
PRINCIPLE: Books are a scandalous temptation relative to visible industry.
SOURCE: Autobiography, wheelbarrow passage
SOURCE CLAIM: A book “sometimes debauch’d me from my work, but that was seldom, snug, and gave no scandal.”
CONTEXT: Credit with merchants.
APPLICATION: Protect deep work from both idleness *and* status-visible leisure.
TENSION: FRANKLIN-004 (read constantly).
COUNTERARGUMENT: Treating study as vice is anti-intellectual.

## 20. Pragmatism

### FRANKLIN-035
PRINCIPLE: If a published principle makes necessary work impossible, people will quit power rather than the principle—or quietly defect.
SOURCE: Autobiography, Quakers vs Dunkers vs fire-company vote
SOURCE CLAIM: Quakers embarrassed by published unlawfulness of all war; Dunkers refused to freeze doctrine; many Quakers absented rather than oppose the battery lottery.
CONTEXT: Defense of the province.
APPLICATION: Write principles with revision paths, or watch hypocrisy.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: Franklin is not a pacifist; he is designing around one.
TENSION: FRANKLIN-006 (virtue precepts he *did* write down).
COUNTERARGUMENT: Pragmatism can gut any ethic that costs.

### FRANKLIN-036
PRINCIPLE: Errata are to be named, not erased from the story.
SOURCE: Autobiography — indenture, Vernon’s money, Deborah Read
SOURCE CLAIM: Asserting freedom against his brother: “not fair,” one of the first errata. Breaking into Vernon’s money: another. Neglect of Miss Read in London: another he would correct if he lived again.
CONTEXT: Self-narrative as moral ledger.
APPLICATION: Keep a failure file; revise the next run.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: Confession in a famous autobiography is also reputation management.
TENSION: FRANKLIN-010 (appearances).
COUNTERARGUMENT: Public errata can be a brand of candor.

## 22. Long-term reputation

### FRANKLIN-039
PRINCIPLE: Institutions that still exist after the founders die are the reputation that matters.
SOURCE: Union Fire Company “upward of fifty years”; Library Company as “mother of” subscription libraries (his phrase in Autobiography tradition)
SOURCE CLAIM: First members dead but one; company flourishes; equipment from fines.
CONTEXT: Old man writing the Autobiography.
APPLICATION: Prefer residual organizations over residual fame.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: Survival ≠ justice of the original design.
TENSION: FRANKLIN-003 (hide authorship).
COUNTERARGUMENT: Institutional immortality can fossilize error (his own Dunker point).

## 23. Wealth vs public contribution

### FRANKLIN-040
PRINCIPLE: Private surplus is supposed to fund public goods and science; patent rents are optional and, for him, refused.
SOURCE: Autobiography — retirement from business; Pennsylvania fireplace; Library Company; hospital (Bond’s idea, his newspaper + matching bill)
SOURCE CLAIM: Moderate fortune → electrical experiments → public “laid hold.” Stove gifted in effect to users; Grace made casting profits. Almanac/newspaper made the fortune that funded the rest. He also took a “very profitable jobb” printing the paper money he had argued for.
CONTEXT: Printer to civic founder.
APPLICATION: Sequence: make a surplus, then mutualize the next problem—without pretending the surplus was disinterested.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: Surplus came from Gazette, almanac, and government printing.
TENSION: FRANKLIN-014 (get all you can honestly) vs FRANKLIN-031 (give inventions away); FRANKLIN-048 (paper-money pamphlet then the printing contract).
COUNTERARGUMENT: Elite philanthropy substitutes for tax-funded equality.

## 24. Failure, revision, and humility

### FRANKLIN-041
PRINCIPLE: Governor Keith’s letters of credit never existed—do not underwrite your life on unwritten patronage.
SOURCE: Autobiography, London 1724
SOURCE CLAIM: Promised letters postponed until sailing; none in the bag; stationer: no such person / Riddlesden a rascal. He had kept the plan secret in Philadelphia; a friend who knew Keith might have warned him.
CONTEXT: First attempt to set up as a printer.
APPLICATION: Verify the instrument, not the cordial speech.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: Youth and secrecy both contributed.
TENSION: FRANKLIN-009 (credit) vs trusting a governor’s “appearance of cordiality.”
COUNTERARGUMENT: Some patronage *is* real; over-cynicism misses patrons.

### FRANKLIN-042
PRINCIPLE: Order can remain the failed virtue; the attempt still mends the hand. A speckled ax may be preferred—and that preference is a temptation.
SOURCE: Autobiography, virtue book (written at Passy, 1784)
SOURCE CLAIM: Scheme of Order gave the most trouble; a master must receive people at their hours; papers and places were “extreamly difficult.” He found himself “incorrigible with respect to Order.” Axe anecdote: the man tired of grinding and “liked a speckled ax best.” Franklin almost concluded the same; also feared “foppery in morals.” Yet though he fell far short of perfection, the endeavour made him better and happier—“those who aim at perfect writing by imitating the engraved copies… their hand is mended.” He later omitted the course entirely when voyages interfered, but always carried the little book.
CONTEXT: Daily marks, then ivory leaves, then yearly, then dropped.
APPLICATION: Keep the tracker; do not confuse the failed row with the whole system; notice the “speckled ax” rationalization.
TENSION: FRANKLIN-005 (resolution: perform without fail); FRANKLIN-007 (humility as appearance).
COUNTERARGUMENT: Keeping a failed row is self-absolution; carrying an unused book is a relic.

### FRANKLIN-043
PRINCIPLE: Inquiry that is actually a trap produces victories the cause did not deserve; keep only modest diffidence.
SOURCE: Autobiography — Socratic method, ~age 16 onward
SOURCE CLAIM: Put on “the humble inquirer and doubter”; grew “artful and expert” at drawing superior people into unforeseen concessions, “obtaining victories that neither myself nor my cause always deserved.” Continued some years, then gradually left it, retaining modest diction (“I conceive / I apprehend”). That remaining habit later helped him “inculcate opinions” and “persuade men into measures.”
CONTEXT: Xenophon + Shaftesbury/Collins doubt; later used on Keimer until Keimer would barely answer.
APPLICATION: Distinguish genuine inquiry from rhetorical capture. If you win by entanglement, discount the win.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: He kept the *manner* of humility because it worked as persuasion, not because he became un-proud.
TENSION: FRANKLIN-007 (appearance of humility as civic tool); FRANKLIN-019 (ends of conversation include persuading).
COUNTERARGUMENT: All skilled persuasion looks like a trap to the loser.

### FRANKLIN-044
PRINCIPLE: A matching grant can make opponents vote for charity they think they will not pay—and then make donors give because the gift doubles.
SOURCE: Autobiography, Pennsylvania Hospital, 1751 (idea originally Dr. Thomas Bond’s)
SOURCE CLAIM: Country members thought £2,000 voluntary was “utterly impossible.” He drafted a conditional clause: when contributors raised a stated capital, the speaker must pay £2,000 from the provincial treasurer. Opponents agreed, conceiving they might have “the credit of being charitable without the expense.” Then, soliciting the public, they urged that every donation would be doubled. The clause “work’d both ways.” He remembered no political manoeuvre whose success pleased him more, “or wherein, after thinking of it, I more easily excus’d myself for having made some use of cunning.”
CONTEXT: Hospital for poor sick, city vs country Assembly split.
APPLICATION: Design the yes as credit-without-cost *and* as leverage for the other side—then admit the cunning.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: Requires a legislature that will swallow a blank sum if it can still throw the bill out.
TENSION: FRANKLIN-043; Sincerity precept.
COUNTERARGUMENT: Matching funds can be a trick that launders public money through vanity.

### FRANKLIN-045
PRINCIPLE: Human felicity is mostly little daily advantages, not rare great fortunes—test the small municipal fix first.
SOURCE: Autobiography — street paving, sixpence sweeper, London Craven-street broom experiment; lamps (Clifton’s sample, Franklin’s four-pane design)
SOURCE CLAIM: “Human felicity is produced not so much by great pieces of good fortune that seldom happen, as by little advantages that occur every day.” Poor woman swept a London street in three hours for a shilling; he inferred a strong man could do it in half the time. Philadelphia: paper to each house, sixpence/month, unanimous subscription, then a paving tax. He ascribes the *idea* of lighting the city to John Clifton’s door lamp, not to himself.
CONTEXT: Mud, dust, shops, scavengers.
APPLICATION: Price a recurring nuisance; run a one-block prototype; do not steal the originator’s credit.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: He later doubted the draining-sled part of the London proposal (narrow streets).
TENSION: FRANKLIN-014 (wealth maxims) vs this everyday-utility doctrine; FRANKLIN-003 (hide own authorship) vs honestly naming Clifton and Bond.
COUNTERARGUMENT: “Little advantages” can become a gentleman’s hobby while structural injustice remains.

### FRANKLIN-046
PRINCIPLE: Do not prepare the celebration before the fact is known.
SOURCE: Autobiography, after organizing Braddock’s wagons
SOURCE CLAIM: The two Doctors Bond brought a subscription for fireworks on taking Fort Duquesne. He looked grave: time enough to prepare when they knew they should have occasion to rejoice. “I don’t know that it will not be taken, but I know that the events of war are subject to great uncertainty.” He gave his reasons; the subscription dropped. Bond later said he did not like Franklin’s forebodings.
CONTEXT: Same campaign he had already doubted to Braddock’s face, then gone quiet.
APPLICATION: Separate logistics help from ceremonial certainty.
TENSION: FRANKLIN-023 (he still advanced money and wagons).
COUNTERARGUMENT: Foreboding can look like disloyalty and freeze necessary morale.

### FRANKLIN-047
PRINCIPLE: After composition, restore the unpaid remainder with interest—credit can be rebuilt by over-performance.
SOURCE: Autobiography, Andrew Denham
SOURCE CLAIM: Denham had failed at Bristol, compounded, went to America, made a plentiful fortune, then invited old creditors to an entertainment. When they expected only the treat, each found under his plate an order on a banker for the full unpaid remainder with interest.
CONTEXT: Franklin’s merchant mentor; Denham died 1727 leaving him a small legacy.
APPLICATION: Reputation after failure is a repayment problem, not a rebranding problem.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: Requires later surplus; compounding creditors is not always possible.
TENSION: FRANKLIN-014 (frugality) vs expensive restoration; Keimer’s opposite path (sell without profit, no accounts).
COUNTERARGUMENT: One rich man’s gesture does not generalize; most bankrupts cannot replay the scene.

### FRANKLIN-048
PRINCIPLE: A civic pamphlet can be right *and* a profitable job for the pamphleteer—name both.
SOURCE: Autobiography, *The Nature and Necessity of a Paper Currency*
SOURCE CLAIM: Argued from street evidence (houses inhabited vs “To be let”) against rich creditors’ fear of depreciation. Anonymous pamphlet; House carried the point; friends rewarded him with printing the money—“a very profitable jobb.” Later: utility became evident, quantity grew with trade, “tho’ I now think there are limits beyond which the quantity may be hurtful.”
CONTEXT: Young printer; Junto debate.
APPLICATION: Track who captures the implementation contract. Revise the earlier quantity claim when evidence changes.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: Conflict of interest is explicit in his own telling.
TENSION: FRANKLIN-003 (hide authorship of public schemes) vs taking the printing; FRANKLIN-009 (credit of the wealthy).
COUNTERARGUMENT: Policy for hire; the later “limits” caveat is cheap once the job is done.

### FRANKLIN-049
PRINCIPLE: When you inherit a bottleneck others used against you, do not imitate the meanness.
SOURCE: Autobiography, Bradford and the post-office
SOURCE CLAIM: Bradford, keeping the post-office, was thought the better advertiser; he forbade riders to carry Franklin’s papers. Franklin bribed riders privately. When Franklin later came into Bradford’s situation, he “took care never to imitate it.”
CONTEXT: Newspaper competition.
APPLICATION: Distribution power is a test of character under reversal.
TENSION: He did bribe riders while the underdog—principle after power, not before.
COUNTERARGUMENT: Selective memory of virtue once the monopoly is yours.

### FRANKLIN-050
PRINCIPLE: Do not give too much for the whistle—false estimates of value, not only idleness, produce misery.
SOURCE: Letter commonly titled “The Whistle,” to Madame Brillon, 10 November 1779 (reprinted in the Gutenberg memoirs volume used for this ingestion)
SOURCE CLAIM: Age seven, spent all his coppers on a whistle worth a quarter of the price; family laughed. Later used it as a check on court favor, popularity, miserliness, pleasure, appearance, and a bad marriage. “Great part of the miseries of mankind are brought upon them by the false estimates they have made of the value of things.” He still might ruin himself on “apples of King John” if auctioned.
CONTEXT: Passy, France—personal letter, not the Autobiography civic chapters.
APPLICATION: Price the status object against what it displaces (liberty, friends, health, the capacity to do good).
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: A charming letter is still a moral essay; it does not price structural constraints.
TENSION: FRANKLIN-014 (accumulate); FRANKLIN-024 (never resign office / court); FRANKLIN-010 (appearance).
COUNTERARGUMENT: “Too much for the whistle” can scold ambition that actually builds hospitals and libraries.
