# A. Core worldview

## SISNEY-001 — Organizations are systems

A business should be diagnosed as an interconnected adaptive system, not as isolated departments or individuals.

Application: trace symptoms upstream before fixing local pieces.

Boundary: this is a systems heuristic; the physics analogy should not be mistaken for literal scientific proof.

## SISNEY-002 — Environmental fit creates usable energy

Sisney calls alignment between capability and opportunity **integration**.

Application: a business can be internally elegant and still fail if it is poorly integrated with the market.

## SISNEY-003 — Internal friction consumes scarce energy

He calls maintenance, coordination, decision friction, rework, and internal drag **entropy**.

Application: reduce entropy, but only after identifying what external outcome matters.

Tension: some redundancy, dissent, and experimentation look inefficient but can create resilience or discovery.

## SISNEY-004 — Adaptation outranks static optimization

The right strategy changes as the environment, product, customer, and execution stage change.

Application: never assume today's structure deserves permanence.

# B. PSIU

## SISNEY-005 — Producing

Near-term tangible results. Necessary for converting intention into customer value.

Failure when overused: heroic output, burnout, weak learning.

## SISNEY-006 — Stabilizing

Process, repeatability, consistency, quality.

Failure when premature: bureaucracy before fit.

## SISNEY-007 — Innovating

Opportunity discovery, future orientation, adaptation.

Failure when overused: novelty addiction and unfinished work.

## SISNEY-008 — Unifying

Relationships, shared understanding, cooperation, cohesion.

Failure when overused: consensus or social harmony displacing accountability.

## SISNEY-009 — Match force to stage

The mix of P/S/I/U should depend on what the system currently needs, not on a generic ideal of balance.

# C. Lifecycle strategy

## SISNEY-010 — Product, market, and execution lifecycles must align

A mismatch between the stage of the product, market, and organization creates avoidable risk.

## SISNEY-011 — Early exploration needs innovation

Pilot-stage work should tolerate uncertainty and prioritize discovery.

## SISNEY-012 — Early adoption needs production

Once a customer problem begins to clarify, verifiable results matter more than ideation.

## SISNEY-013 — Scale needs stabilization

Repeatability and process become valuable only after enough demand/utility has been demonstrated.

## SISNEY-014 — Read the stage with evidence

Sisney publicly uses market growth, competition, pricing pressure, and net cash flow as stage indicators.

Boundary: no single metric determines stage.

# D. Structure

## SISNEY-015 — Structure follows strategy and stage

If strategy or lifecycle changes materially, structure should change.

## SISNEY-016 — Short-range functions should not dominate long-range ones

Immediate execution can crowd out future opportunity if reporting/control is poorly designed.

## SISNEY-017 — Efficiency should not dominate effectiveness

Optimizing cost/process can destroy the ability to choose and create the right thing.

## SISNEY-018 — Central control and local autonomy must coexist

Too much control bottlenecks; too little creates systemic risk.

## SISNEY-019 — Role fit matters

People should be placed where their working style and the role's demands fit.

## SISNEY-020 — Process animates structure

An org chart is inert without decision and work flows.

# E. Designed to Scale

Official source: https://organizationalphysics.com/books/designed-to-scale/

## SISNEY-021 — Design for the next stage, not the last one

The book focuses on structural traps that appear as organizations grow.

## SISNEY-022 — Role clarity and accountability reduce drag

Ambiguous ownership creates coordination cost and delayed decisions.

## SISNEY-023 — Cross-functional flow matters more than silo elegance

Recent Sisney writing sharpens this into a horizontal-first structural principle: customer journey, product development, employee/agent experience, and strategic execution cut across vertical departments.

Important terminology note:

> Sisney's **horizontal** here means end-to-end flows across the organization; **vertical** means functional/accountability structure. It is not the same as horizontal versus vertical market exploration.

Do not reuse these words across contexts without defining them.

## SISNEY-024 — The founder should migrate from doing to designing

Scale requires spending more time on the system than personally executing every task.

# F. Hiring

## SISNEY-025 — Hire for role/system fit, not generic excellence

Talent is contextual. A strong person in the wrong role can underperform.

## SISNEY-026 — Build complementary teams

PSIU differences can be useful when the work requires different forces.

Boundary: do not reduce hiring to a personality code; demonstrated competence, incentives, values, and references still matter.

# G. AI-era operating doctrine

## SISNEY-027 — AI multiplies the system already present

Good structure can be accelerated by AI; bad structure can generate more entropy faster.

Source: 2025–2026 AI structure essays.

## SISNEY-028 — Design. Delegate. Design.

Modern leadership increasingly means specifying outcome, quality, constraints, safeguards, and feedback; delegating execution; then redesigning the system from observed results.

The key move is not vague delegation. It is precise system design followed by recursive revision.

## SISNEY-029 — Craft becomes delegable faster than judgment

Sisney's 2026 framing places more leader value in system design, judgment, direction, context, and accountability as execution becomes easier to delegate.

## SISNEY-030 — Type 1 / Type 2 AI governance

High-impact, difficult-to-reverse decisions remain human; lower-risk reversible decisions can increasingly be delegated as capability and controls improve.

## SISNEY-031 — Proprietary context becomes strategic

His recent AI writing argues that access to the same frontier models does not create differentiation; coherent proprietary organizational context does.

Tension: centralized context can improve coherence but can also create concentration, surveillance, security, or single-point-of-failure risk.

## SISNEY-032 — Rules are scaffolding; principles are durable

In fast-moving AI systems, model-specific rules decay. Build around stable principles and revise the harness as capabilities change.

## SISNEY-033 — Sensemaking is not merely routing

Agents can automate routing and information movement, but strategic sensemaking remains a shared human practice in his 2026 view.

A flat organization does not eliminate the need for leadership judgment; it may increase it.

## SISNEY-034 — One accountable owner for important initiatives

Sisney advocates a clear single-threaded owner / implementer for major AI transformation efforts, supported by cross-functional context and recurring review.

## SISNEY-035 — AI harnesses can become choke points

A customized wrapper that is too tightly coupled to current model limitations may become obsolete as base models improve.

Application:

Design orchestration around enduring business principles, replaceable model layers, and explicit context—not tricks that exist only because today's model is weak.

# I. Important tensions

1. **Physics metaphor vs empirical precision** — memorable first-principles frame, but not a literal physics science of firms.
2. **Alignment vs variation** — coherence accelerates execution; dissent can prevent coordinated error.
3. **Low entropy vs useful slack** — not all redundancy or exploration is waste.
4. **Single owner vs collective sensemaking** — accountability can be singular while interpretation remains collaborative.
5. **Horizontal flow vs vertical accountability** — end-to-end customer flow and clear functional ownership must coexist.
6. **AI central brain vs decentralization** — shared context can improve coordination while creating concentration risk.
7. **Lifecycle maps vs nonlinear reality** — stages clarify sequence but real businesses can straddle stages.
8. **PSIU vocabulary vs labeling bias** — useful as a diagnostic language, dangerous as identity essentialism.
9. **Delegation vs learning** — delegating craft can free leadership; premature delegation can remove contact with reality.
10. **Structure vs market** — excellent organization cannot compensate for no customer pull.

# J. Lex Sisney shorthand

> **See the whole system, read the stage, align structure to strategy, reduce avoidable entropy, and redesign continuously as reality changes.**

For AI:

> **Design → Delegate → Observe → Redesign. Human judgment and context remain accountable.**
