# Lens: Superforecasting

Status: active (ingestion #1)
Source: `wisdom/books/SUPERFORECASTING.md`

Use for uncertain opportunities, forecasts, strategy, PMF search, geopolitics, and decisions where confidence can be updated with evidence.

---

## 1. Define the question

Turn fuzzy claims into resolvable questions.

Bad: “This market is promising.”

Better: “Will at least one of 20 qualified prospects pay $300 for this pilot within 14 days?”

## 2. Outside view first

What usually happens in comparable cases?

Name the reference class and base rate before telling the special story.

## 3. Probability

How likely is the outcome, numerically?

Use ranges when precision is weak. The point is explicit uncertainty, not false exactness.

## 4. Decompose

Break one big belief into the few uncertainties that could actually kill it:

- desirability
- feasibility
- viability
- survivability / adaptability

## 5. Disconfirm

What evidence says the opposite?

Search deliberately for substitutes, failures, blockers, weak payers, bad economics, and reasons generic AI may erase the edge.

## 6. Pre-mortem

Assume the conclusion was wrong. Why?

Also imagine unexpectedly strong success. What did we initially miss?

## 7. Update

Record:

**prior → evidence → posterior → why**

Do not defend yesterday’s probability.

## 8. Calibration

Make predictions before outcomes are known and resolve them honestly.

Over time ask: when we say 70%, are we right roughly 70% of the time?

## 9. Decision threshold

A forecast is useful only if it changes action.

Consider probability together with upside, downside, reversibility, cost, and information gained by testing.

## 10. Market beats forecast

Forecasting selects and sizes bets. It does not replace real-world evidence.

---

## Kill questions

- What exactly are we predicting?
- What is the relevant base rate?
- Which assumption kills the idea if false?
- What would make us lower our probability materially?
- What is the strongest outside case against us?
- Are we interpreting clicks as demand, or demand as payment?
- Did we write the prediction before seeing the result?
- What result would make us stop?
- Are we forecasting forever instead of running a cheap reversible test?

Shorthand:

> **Believe in probabilities. Test in reality. Update without ego.**
